# Reconnect with someone

Each morning, picks one contact you have not spoken to in a while and drafts a
short check-in.

!!! quote "Say this in chat"

    Every weekday morning, pick one contact I haven't been in touch with for
    over three months, and draft a short, friendly check-in email to them —
    reference whatever we last talked about. Put it in my approvals queue, don't
    send it.

**Needs:** contacts or a CRM, and mail.

## Why it works

Staying in touch fails because it is nobody's job on any particular day. There
is never a moment when reconnecting with someone is the most urgent thing you
could be doing, so it never happens.

One draft a morning, already written, turns it into a ten-second decision:
send, edit, or skip. The work of remembering *who* and *what you last discussed*
is already done, and that was the expensive part.

## Worth adjusting

**If the drafts feel generic**, say *"only pick people we've actually had a real
conversation with"*. It will be pickier about who it chooses, which matters more
than the wording — a warm note to someone you barely know reads worse than
silence.

**If three months is wrong for your work**, change it. Six months suits a long
sales cycle; one month suits an active network.

**If you get too many**, make it weekly. One good draft a week that you actually
send beats five a day you skip.

## Do not auto-send this one

!!! warning

    This is the recipe where approval matters most. These go to real people, in
    your name, referencing a shared history the assistant reconstructed. Most
    will be fine. The one that is not — wrong context, wrong tone, someone you
    fell out with — is the one you would very much want to have caught.

    Keep it in the approvals queue.
