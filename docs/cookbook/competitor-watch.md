# Competitor watch

Once a week, checks the companies you care about and tells you what actually
changed — new pages, new pricing, new announcements — rather than making you go
and look.

!!! quote "Say this in chat"

    Every Monday at 8am, check the websites and recent news for Acme, Northwind
    and Initech. Tell me what's changed since last week — new products, pricing
    changes, announcements, anything on their blog. Only tell me what's new,
    don't re-summarise things you've already reported.

**Needs:** web access.

## Why it works

Competitor research is something everyone agrees is valuable and nobody does
weekly, because each individual check is boring and the answer is usually
"nothing". The value is entirely in the accumulation — you want to know about
the pricing change in the week it happened, not six months later from a customer.

Delegating it costs you one email a week and catches the week that mattered.

## "Only tell me what's new" is the important phrase

!!! tip

    Without it, you get the same summary of Acme's homepage every Monday, and by
    week three you stop reading — at which point the automation is worse than
    useless, because you now believe you are covered.

    That phrase leans on [memory](../memory/index.md): it can only tell you what
    changed if it recalls what it said last time. Worth confirming it is working
    by checking the second week's email actually differs from the first.

## Worth adjusting

**Name the companies explicitly.** "My competitors" makes it guess, and it will
guess differently each week — which quietly breaks the change detection.

**Say which pages matter.** Pricing and changelog pages change meaningfully;
homepages change constantly and mean nothing. *"Watch their pricing and blog,
ignore the marketing copy"* gets you a much better signal.

**Add news if you want it**, but expect noise. Site changes are factual; news
coverage is a lot of the same press release rewritten.

## Adjacent: watch one specific thing

The same shape works narrower, and narrower is often better:

> Check Acme's pricing page every Monday and tell me only if the numbers changed.

That one is nearly noise-free, and it is the kind of thing you would genuinely
never remember to do.
