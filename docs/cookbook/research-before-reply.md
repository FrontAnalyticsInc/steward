# Research before you reply

When a message arrives from someone you have not dealt with before, get a short
brief on who they are — before you write back, not after.

!!! quote "Say this in chat"

    When an email arrives from someone I've never corresponded with before, look
    them up — their company, their role, what the company does — and send me a
    short brief alongside the message. Don't reply to them, just tell me.

**Needs:** mail and web access. Better with contacts or a CRM.

## Why it works

The first reply sets the relationship, and it is the one you write with the least
information. You either answer quickly and generically, or you spend ten minutes
searching before answering an email that might be nothing.

This removes the choice. The context arrives with the message, so a considered
first reply costs the same as a generic one.

## Where it pays most

**Inbound enquiries.** Knowing whether you are talking to a two-person startup or
a division of something large changes the entire reply.

**Introductions.** "Sarah suggested I get in touch" is much easier to answer well
when you know what Sarah's contact actually does.

**Anything you would otherwise skim.** The brief makes it obvious in five seconds
whether a message deserves ten minutes.

## Worth adjusting

**"Never corresponded with before" is the useful filter.** Without it, you get a
brief on everyone including people you speak to daily, and you stop reading
them.

**Say what you care about.** *"Their role and whether their company looks like a
fit for us"* gives a far more useful brief than an open-ended lookup.

**Exclude the obvious.** *"Skip newsletters, receipts and automated mail"* — those
are all technically new senders.

## Pair it with a draft

Once the brief is useful, the natural extension:

> Same thing, but also draft a reply based on what you found. Leave it in my
> approvals queue.

Now the whole first response is prepared — brief and draft — and your job is to
read both and decide.

!!! warning "Keep this one in the approvals queue"

    A first reply to someone you have never met, written from a web search the
    assistant did on them, is exactly the message you want to read before it
    goes. Getting a detail wrong about who someone is reads far worse than
    replying slowly.
