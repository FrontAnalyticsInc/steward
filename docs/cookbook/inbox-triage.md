# Inbox triage

Sorts new mail as it arrives and drafts replies to anything that clearly needs
one. Nothing sends without you.

!!! quote "Say this in chat"

    Check my inbox every 15 minutes. Sort anything new by whether it needs a
    reply from me, and draft replies for the ones that do. Don't send anything —
    leave the drafts for me to approve.

**Needs:** mail connected.

## Why it works

The slow part of email is not writing, it is deciding. Opening a message,
working out whether it needs you, and remembering the context costs more than
typing the reply does.

This does the deciding and hands you a first draft, so your job becomes editing
and approving — which is a different, much faster kind of work.

## Start at 15 minutes, not 1

!!! warning

    Every run costs something and produces something for you to review. A
    one-minute schedule on a busy inbox generates more review work than it
    saves, and you end up rubber-stamping — which defeats the point of approval.

    Start at 15 minutes. Tighten it only if you find it genuinely lagging behind
    your mail.

## Worth adjusting

**If it drafts replies to things that did not need one**, say so: *"stop
drafting replies to newsletters and automated notifications"*.

**If the drafts are too formal**, say that too. One sentence in chat changes
every future draft — much better than rejecting them one at a time.

**If you only want the sorting**, drop the drafting: *"just tell me what needs a
reply, don't write anything"*.

## Where the drafts go

Into the approvals queue, not your Drafts folder. See
[Reviewing its work](../automations/review.md).

If the automation ran but you cannot find anything, check there first — it is
almost always the answer.
