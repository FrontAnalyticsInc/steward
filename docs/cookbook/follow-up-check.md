# Weekly follow-up check

Once a week, tells you who you left hanging. It changes nothing — this is a
report, not an action.

!!! quote "Say this in chat"

    Every Monday at 9am, look through the last 30 days of mail for messages
    still sitting unread in my inbox that I never replied to. Send me the list
    with a one-line summary of each. Don't change anything — just tell me.

**Needs:** mail connected.

## Why it works

The messages that damage relationships are not the ones you said no to. They are
the ones you never answered — and those are invisible *precisely because* you
never opened them. They do not appear in your sent mail, they do not appear in
your tasks, and scrolling your inbox does not surface them because they look
like everything else.

A weekly list is enough. Daily turns it into noise you stop reading.

## "Don't change anything" is the important phrase

!!! tip

    Saying it explicitly keeps this a report rather than something that starts
    filing your mail. Without it, an assistant asked to deal with unanswered
    email may reasonably decide that archiving, labelling or replying is what
    you wanted.

    Include that phrase in any recipe where you want a recommendation and not an
    action. It is the single most useful sentence in this cookbook.

## Worth adjusting

**If the list is too long the first time**, that is normal — it is catching a
month's backlog. Either work through it once, or narrow it: *"only ones from
actual people, not mailing lists"*.

**If 30 days is wrong**, change it. Two weeks keeps it urgent; ninety days turns
it into an audit.
