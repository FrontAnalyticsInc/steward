# Morning briefing

The one to start with. An email each morning with what is on your calendar, plus
what the assistant knows about the people you are meeting.

!!! quote "Say this in chat"

    Every weekday at 7am, email me a briefing for the day: everything on my
    calendar, and for each meeting, who I'm meeting and anything you know about
    them or their company. Keep it short — I read it on my phone.

**Needs:** calendar, and a way to email you. Better with a CRM connected.

## Why it works

It collapses three things you would otherwise do by hand — open the calendar,
recognise the names, remember the context — into something you read in a minute
while the kettle boils.

The value is in the second half. A list of meetings you already know about is not
worth an email; a list of meetings *with who those people are* is.

## Worth adjusting

**If it is too long**, say "just the meetings and one line each". Length is the
usual complaint and it is a one-sentence fix.

**If the weekend annoys you**, "every weekday" already handles it — but check the
schedule it confirms, because "every morning" quietly includes Sunday.

**If you want it earlier**, remember the briefing is only useful if it arrives
before you start your day, not while you are in the first meeting.

## Safe to auto-send

This is one of the few recipes where turning off approval is reasonable — it is
addressed to you, and nobody else sees it. Say *"you don't need to ask before
sending this one"* when you set it up.
